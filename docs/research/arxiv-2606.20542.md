## 1. Concise source summary

**Paper claims (Abstract; §1):** the paper introduces the Caltech Tennis Dataset (CalTennis), a large-scale multi-view video benchmark for evaluating monocular-to-3D pose estimation "in the wild," plus a label-free evaluation methodology and a benchmark of five state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods. The central thesis is that synchronized overlapping views let reconstruction error be lower-bounded by cross-view disagreement without MOCAP, body sensors, or manual annotation, and that this exposes failure modes invisible to existing benchmarks.

**Paper claims — data (§3.1–§3.2, Table 1, Fig. 1–2):** over 11 million frames (51 hours) of tennis practice and match play from 40 players (Caltech men's/women's teams plus recreational players), captured with 2–6 synchronized consumer cameras at 60 Hz in HD (1920×1080, iPhone 14 or newer main camera, 1.65 m MagSafe tripods at ~$40 each, cameras ~12 m apart at 1.65 m height, standard court geometry). Per-configuration frame counts range 0.4M–3.3M (Fig. 1). Sessions were IRB-approved with voluntary consent and faces blurred with the `deface` algorithm. The paper claims 10× more frames than prior in-the-wild benchmarks and 3× more than the largest MOCAP-ground-truthed dataset, 90% of poses at 13.4–16.7 m depth (vs. 4.5–5.8 m in Human3.6M), 85% pose-space coverage (vs. 62% next-best real-world), and highest articulation on knees/shoulders/elbows (Table 1; §3.2; Fig. 11–12, App. A.5).

**Paper claims — method (§4, App. A.1–A.2, Figs. 3/8):** cameras are calibrated from standardized court-line intersections via PnP reprojection-error minimization (Eq. 1/14); per-view SMPL-X estimates (translation τ, orientation φ, body pose θ, shape β) are lifted to a shared court frame via a model-to-world transform (Eq. 15–16); an MLE precision-weighted consensus pose down-weights each camera's depth axis (Eq. 7–13 / Eq. 17, App. A.1). Temporal sync is two-stage: linear interpolation of reconstructed poses to arbitrary timestamps plus grid-search of a global offset Δt ∈ [−1000, +1000] ms minimizing cross-view pose disagreement, motivated by iPhone wall-clock logging at 1-second granularity (§4; App. A.2).

**Paper claims — models benchmarked (§6, App. A.4, Table 4):** five SOTA monocular-to-3D methods — TRAM, GVHMR, GENMO (video diffusion), WHAM (foot-contact refinement), PromptHMR (prompt-conditioned transformer) — run without supplied camera coordinates (each runs its own camera estimation), on an NVIDIA H100, on the first 5M frames ("full results forthcoming," §6). Prior-benchmark numbers for the same models are reproduced in Table 4 (e.g., PA-MPJPE ~34–49 mm on 3DPW/EMDB/RICH).

**Paper claims — evaluation (§5–§6, Tables 2/7, Figs. 4–7/9–10/16–17):** standard multi-view consistency metrics (translation L2 Eq. 2; pelvis-relative pose error Eq. 3; MPJPE/PA-MPJPE/PVE) plus two novel metrics — footwork (foot-velocity disagreement and foot-height disagreement, Eq. 4) and stability (distance of projected center of mass to convex hull of grounded feet, zero if inside, Eq. 5; cross-view disagreement Eq. 6) — plus qualitative shape-consistency study (Fig. 7). Headline result (Table 2; §6.1; §7): no single model dominates; PromptHMR leads translation/pose/PA-MPJPE (0.942 m / 105 mm / 84 mm) but is worst on foot velocity (3.23 m/s vs. WHAM 0.72 m/s); WHAM leads foot velocity; GENMO leads foot height (60 mm) and stability (16 mm); translation errors span 0.9–3.6 m with "pose drifting" along depth axes; pose errors cluster ~105–115 mm. Author interpretation (§7): joint-angle recovery is now "quite accurate" and usable with care for activity recognition / coarse technique analysis, but absolute depth, ground-contact detection, and body shape (limb lengths, height, proportions) are unreliable in every model and unsuitable for clinical/forensic/force-balance use.

## 2. Data / method / model / evaluation limits (paper claims unless noted)

- **Label-free means lower bound only.** §7 and Limitations state explicitly that multi-view disagreement lower-bounds error and complements rather than replaces absolute MOCAP validation. *Project inference:* a low disagreement score must not be read as absolute accuracy; a high score is evidence of error.
- **Partial-dataset benchmark.** §6 states metrics are computed on the first 5M of 11M frames with full results forthcoming. No train/test split exists (methods are pre-trained and evaluated zero-shot); no per-serve, per-player, or per-session breakdown is given in the main tables.
- **Serve-agnostic.** Serves are part of the "rich action vocabulary (serves, volleys, sprints)" (§1) and footwork/stroke examples, but there are no serve-phase labels, no Kovacs stages, no toss/contact/finish timestamps, no serve-type or handedness labels, and no ball/racket observations. Nothing is timed as an event.
- **Single sport/climate/surface/group.** Limitations (§7): one sport, one climate/surface type, one collector group; the "easy and inexpensive" replication claim is unverified by other teams. Privacy relies on IRB + consent + face blurring.
- **Unsynchronized consumer timestamps.** The ±1000 ms iPhone offset (§4; App. A.2) is corrected by minimizing the very pose disagreement being measured — a circularity risk the paper does not quantify with an independent sync ground truth (e.g., clap/flash). *Project inference:* reported disagreements conflate residual sync error with model error to an unknown degree.
- **Coarse correspondence.** Person matching across views ("matched persons," Eq. 6) and foot-joint sets K_foot are assumed without a stated re-ID or association protocol; TRAM's errors are later attributed in App. A.9 to "identity-level failure modes (re-identification failures, trajectory breaks)" — consistent with this gap.
- **Shape/stability definitions depend on the models under test.** Stability uses each model's own CoM and grounded-foot estimates (Eq. 5); shape consistency is qualitative (Fig. 7) with no numeric shape metric in Table 2. *Project inference:* these are disagreement diagnostics, not calibrated biomechanical measurements.
- **Failure-analysis scope.** App. A.9 defines failure as top-30% error per model, ten hand-built scene features (Table 6), Spearman correlations and L1-logistic AUC (Table 7, Figs. 16–17). Motion magnitude/acceleration correlations are weak (|r_s| < 0.17) — interpreted by the authors as compressed motion distribution in sports, not as proof that speed is irrelevant in general.
- **No ball, racket, audio, or contact detector.** Ball position/velocity, racket pose, audio transients, and impact timing never appear as inputs, outputs, or metrics. Contact-adjacent quantities are feet/ground only.

## 3. Findings relevant to pose features (angles / speeds) and phase / event detection

### 3.1 Pose features: joint angles are the bright spot; depth/scale are the failure

- **Paper claims:** pelvis-relative pose error is ~105–115 mm across all five models (Table 2; Fig. 4 left) and PA-MPJPE 84–119 mm, versus translation error 942–3587 mm — i.e., relative configuration is an order of magnitude more consistent than absolute position. Per-joint articulation analysis (§3.2; Fig. 12) finds CalTennis highest on knees/shoulders/elbows. Joint-error correlations (Fig. 10, App. A.3) show upper-body joints correlate with each other and lower-body with each other (plus pelvis), but upper vs. lower errors are nearly uncorrelated; torso joints align with lower body for PromptHMR but upper body for GVHMR.
- **Project inference:** this corroborates the M4.2/remediation choice to build stage evidence from normalized, visibility-gated 2D configurations (elbow/knee angles, shoulder/hip tilts, shoulder–hip separation, wrist trajectories) rather than absolute image positions or depth-dependent quantities, and to require multiple cues per stage (M4.3) rather than one global extremum. The upper/lower decorrelation specifically supports per-region evidence and independent quality channels: a consistent lower body must not vouch for an occluded wrist, and vice versa. No paper constant (weight, threshold, window) transfers to M4.

### 3.2 Speeds: velocities are evaluated only as foot agreement; raw acceleration is excluded by design

- **Paper claims:** the only velocity signals are foot-joint velocities/heights in the footwork metric (Eq. 4): WHAM 0.72 m/s vs. PromptHMR 3.23 m/s foot-velocity disagreement; GENMO best foot-height (60 mm). The M5-adjacent design detail in §3.2 of the *M5 proposal* (not this paper) excludes raw second derivatives; this paper's own pipeline smooths only via each model's internal temporal processing and linear pose interpolation for sync (App. A.2), with no stated Savitzky–Golay windows or physical-delta derivative formulation. Motion/acceleration scene features are weak failure predictors (Table 7; App. A.9).
- **Project inference:** the paper offers no usable wrist-speed estimator, no smoothing window in seconds, and no cadence finding (all data are 60 Hz; our contract is native 120 fps). Its lesson is negative and structural: foot-speed agreement collapses without explicit contact reasoning (WHAM's refinement step is the authors' hypothesis for its lead, §6.1), so M4 must not claim foot-contact, weight-transfer, or speed-derived events from body pose alone. Our `start`/`finish` velocity-run rules and 50%-arc-length `acceleration`/`deceleration` midpoints (remediation §3) remain unvalidated by this paper; at most the foot-velocity spread (0.72–6.65 m/s across models, Table 2) cautions that any speed threshold must be attempt-relative, visibility-qualified, and uncertainty-floored, as remediation §4 already requires.

### 3.3 Phase / event detection: none — no stages, no decoder, no timing errors

- **Paper claims:** there are no phases, events, intervals, keyframes, ordering constraints, duration priors, DP/Viterbi decoding, or Hit@ms / timing-error metrics anywhere in the paper. The "events" are frames; the "sequence" is the whole clip. Eval horizons do not exist; outputs are dense per-frame SMPL-X tracks, not sparse timestamps. The eight Kovacs stages (`start`, `release`, `loading`, `cocking`, `acceleration`, `contact`, `deceleration`, `finish`) have no counterpart.
- **Project inference:** this paper contributes nothing directly to eight-stage sequencing, contact anchoring, or phase-evaluation methodology (availability, accepted-keyframe rate, signed/absolute error, P90, order violations, calibration). Do not cite Table 2 as a phase-accuracy reference; translation/pose millimeters and foot-velocity m/s are inter-view disagreements, not stage-timing errors in seconds.

### 3.4 Contact, release, ball–racket claims: absent

- **Paper claims:** ball–racket impact is never detected, timestamped, or evaluated; toss release never appears; racket position/orientation never appears (racket/ball work is absent even from related work). The only "contact" is foot–ground contact agreement (Eq. 4–6, Table 2, Fig. 5), which is inconsistent across frames and views in all models (§6.1).
- **Project inference:** the foot-contact inconsistency is the closest available corroboration for our contract: `contact` stays an audio-anchored uncertain interval (M4.1, remediation §3), `release`/`cocking` stay labeled body proxies with explicit limitations, and no visual ball–racket contact or ball-derived release rule may be claimed in M4. The paper's footwork/stability failures also support keeping foot-dependent quantities (landing/recovery quality, balance, force) out of M4 outputs per design §2.

## 4. What M4 can adopt or evaluate (no new models / dependencies / ball–racket claims)

All items fit the M4.1–M4.6 + remediation allowed files (feature/evidence/solver diagnostics and report wording) and require no new packages, weights, or ball/racket observation. None changes M3 outputs.

1. **Keep scale-normalized, camera-relative 2D descriptors; do not add depth/shape.** *Paper claims:* depth drives MPJPE failures (Spearman up to +0.54, scale proxy −0.44, Table 7/Fig. 16; AUC to 0.89 for GENMO), while PA-MPJPE (translation-removed) failures are weakly predictable (max |r_s| ≤ 0.21, App. A.9). *Project inference:* retain torso-normalized coordinates, attempt-relative normalization, and camera-relative naming; never call camera-axis motion anatomical posterior/forward; never emit height/limb-length/shape claims. This is process corroboration only.
2. **Keep explicit uncertainty/observation handling; down-weight uncertain axes.** *Paper claims:* the MLE consensus (App. A.1) elongates per-camera covariance along the depth axis and precision-weights accordingly (Eq. 7–13). *Project inference:* analogous discipline already exists in M4.2 (observed/quality/interpolation-span channels, short interpolation only, long gaps forbidden, uncertainty never narrower than observation spacing). No covariance value transfers; the transferable point is to weight evidence by observation quality and forbid interpolated points as sole support for high-confidence keyframes (M4.3).
3. **Keep multi-cue, per-region stage evidence.** *Paper claims:* upper/lower error decorrelation (Fig. 10) and model-specific failure sets (Jaccard ≤ 0.30, Figs. 16–17; §6.1 "no single model dominates"). *Project inference:* when building the phase-debug artifact (remediation §5), verify each body stage combines ≥2 visibility-qualified cues and that per-region quality is independent; do not let one model's (or one region's) confidence vouch for another. Treat any single-extremum stage definition as suspect.
4. **Keep physical-time handling.** *Paper claims:* millisecond-level sync via interpolation + Δt search (§4; App. A.2); 60 Hz capture with exact device timestamps; smoothing/evaluation discussed in physical units. *Project inference:* consistent with remediation §4 (exact presentation timestamps, timestamp deltas, second-based windows, never `frame / assumed_fps`). No constant to import.
5. **Adopt the paper's reporting discipline in private diagnostics.** *Paper claims:* per-model × per-metric breakdowns (Table 2), median + 25th/75th percentile bars (Fig. 4), per-video histograms (Fig. 6), and scene-feature failure analysis (Table 7) rather than a single mean score. *Project inference:* mirror the habit in debug notes — per-stage availability, signed/absolute error, P90, order violations, contact-relative offsets, and observation support at manual vs. selected times (remediation §5 already requires this). Do not change the committed gate; optionally add a trivial contact-centered naive baseline in notes to avoid fooling ourselves on easy stages.
6. **Use court geometry only as documentation context, not as an M4 input.** *Paper claims:* PnP calibration from court intersections (Eq. 1/14) enables the shared frame. *Project inference:* no M4 leaf may add a calibration stage; the citable point is that our rear-view-only contract deliberately forgoes the multi-view/world-frame machinery this paper needs, so world-coordinate quantities (translation, CoM, foot height in meters) are unavailable by design in M4.

## 5. What cannot fit M4 (and why)

- **SMPL/SMPL-X 3D lifting or any new pose backend.** M4 remediation pins the existing approved MediaPipe Heavy 2D body model + raw-source audio (remediation §1). New weights need new dependencies and a Core ML path per design §6, allowed only in the ticket that first uses them. The paper itself warns depth/shape/foot estimates from such models are unstable (§7; Table 2; Fig. 7) — a swap would add scope without fixing the diagnosed chronology failure.
- **Multi-view capture, calibration, or consensus fusion (Eq. 7–17, App. A.1–A.2).** M4 contract is one right-handed rear-view iPhone source at native 120 fps (remediation §1). Stereo rigs, PnP calibration, Δt grid search, and precision-weighted fusion belong to no M4 leaf and would alter the operating contract.
- **Footwork / stability / shape metrics (Eq. 4–6, Table 2, Figs. 5/7) as outputs.** Each requires world-frame 3D, CoM, grounded-foot sets, and shape parameters M4 does not observe and must not claim (M4.1 rubric constraints; design §2). Reporting them from 2D pose alone would be an unsupported claim.
- **Learned scorers, duration priors, or training (any TCN/diffusion/transformer).** Roadmap M4.1–M4.4 mandate deterministic evidence + DP solver; remediation §8 defers all training and PyTorch. The paper trains nothing new (it benchmarks pre-trained models) and provides no learned phase scorer to copy.
- **Ball/racket tracking or visual contact.** Absent from the paper and explicitly deferred (remediation §8). Foot-contact refinement (WHAM, §6.1) is a 3D-model-internal step, not a ball–racket detector, and its translation penalty (2.664 m, Table 2) underscores that contact reasoning trades off against other axes.
- **Dataset-scale collection or H100 evaluation.** The 11M-frame corpus, Hugging Face release, and 5M-frame H100 protocol (§§3/6) are not reproducible inside the narrow heuristic/chronology repair and would be new scope.
- **Speed estimation, coaching, technique scoring, 3D biomechanics.** The paper attempts none as outputs and explicitly deems current models unsuitable for force/balance/clinical use (§7); design §2 and M4.1 exclude them regardless.

## 6. What belongs in deferred M5 (and why)

Per `docs/m5-tcn-phase-detection.md` (proposal) and remediation §8:

- **Learned temporal scorer over dense native features (M5 §3.3 TCN + §3.4 DP decoder).** *Why M5:* the paper's core forward lesson is that hand-built per-frame estimates fail systematically on depth/contact/shape while relative kinematics remain informative — the same gap a learned multi-cue scorer with modality dropout and augmentation (M5 §3.3) is designed to address. The paper motivates sequence learning in general; it does not specify the M5 heatmap-event formulation (Gaussian σ 20–50 ms, 120 Hz grid, observation mask), which remains proposal-only.
- **Ball (and racket) channels.** *Why M5-or-later:* absent here, so no specification to copy; M5 §3 is the first place ball position/velocity/confidence channels appear, gated separately and never automatic ground truth.
- **Multi-view / multi-rate / multi-style generalization.** *Why M5:* paper spans 2–6 views, ~5–30 m depths (App. A.9), varied strokes/footwork, and 60 Hz sources; our M4 contract is single rear-view 120 fps. Cross-view conditioning, handedness flags, and 30–240 fps canonical-grid handling are M5 §§1–3 scope requiring the labeling and session-disjoint evaluation M4 must not attempt. The phone+tripod+court-geometry capture recipe (§3.1; Fig. 15; App. A.8) is at most a pointer for how an M5 collection *could* be done cheaply — not an M4 action.
- **Foot-contact / stability / shape-aware evaluation.** *Why M5-or-later:* M5 Phase 4 (Hit@25/50/100 ms, P90/P95, ablations) could consider foot-height/stability-style consistency diagnostics alongside timing metrics once a 3D-capable, calibrated pipeline exists. In M4 they are unobservable and forbidden as claims.
- **3D lifting as downstream metric engine only.** *Why downstream:* both this paper (§7; App. A.5–A.7) and M5 §5 agree monocular 3D degrades on high-speed athletic motion and must not drive primary timing. Any 3D use stays behind the phase gate as an analytics experiment, never as a contact/shape claim.
- **Anything needing PyTorch, new weights, training data, or annotation GUI/dataset work (M5 §6 Phases 2–3).** *Why deferred:* remediation §8 names these explicitly; the paper's scale (80–120 serves in M5 §4 vs. 11M frames here) shows how far a minimal learned experiment is from a benchmark corpus — do not conflate them.

If M4 succeeds narrowly, its dense 120 fps caches and diagnostics become *proposed* labels for M5 review (remediation §8) — the same provisional status any CalTennis-derived pseudo-label would hold.

## 7. Prioritized recommendations

1. **No M4 threshold, weight, or window changes from this paper.** It has no serve stages, no usable speed estimator, and no transferable constants — our failure is eight-stage chronology on 120 fps rear-view slow motion, not 3D reconstruction on 60 Hz multi-view tennis. Changing evidence weights from Table 2/7 would be tuning to an incomparable task.
2. **Use the paper once, as a negative-constraint check in the debug artifact:** confirm no stage depends on absolute depth/scale, foot contact, CoM/balance, or body shape; confirm long-gap stages go `partial`/`unavailable` with stable anomaly IDs instead of being infilled; confirm per-region quality is independent (Fig. 10 lesson). If any check fails, the fault is in candidate generation or skip scoring (remediation §5).
3. **Keep the audio-anchored `contact` with uncertainty and the `release`/`cocking` proxy labels.** The paper's foot-contact inconsistency (§6.1; Table 2) and shape inconsistency (Fig. 7) are the strongest external corroboration for refusing visual-contact and shape claims from body pose — cite them as such, not as validation.
4. **Do not adopt SMPL/SMPL-X lifting, PnP court calibration, multi-view fusion, footwork/stability outputs, or any learned scorer in M4.** Each violates the narrow contract, adds dependencies, or makes ball/racket/shape claims the paper itself shows are unreliable.
5. **File narrow M5 pointers:** learned multi-cue dynamics, ball channels, cross-view/rate generalization, cheap phone-tripod collection recipe, and foot/shape-aware diagnostics go to M5; cite this paper's "no single best model" (§7; Figs. 16–17) and depth-dependence findings (Table 7) as motivation, not as a specification.
