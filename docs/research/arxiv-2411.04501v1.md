## 1. Concise source summary

**Paper claims:** the paper proposes Pose2Trajectory, an encoder–decoder Transformer that predicts a tennis player's future image-plane trajectory (centroid X,Y sequence) from past body-joint positions, past centroid history, and ball position (Abstract; §4.4, Figure 4). The stated application is broadcast production: giving close-up cameras 0.5–1.0 s of predicted player location so they can auto-track without human intervention (§1; Figure 1 shows 15/30/60-frame-ahead 224×224 boxes at 250 ms / 500 ms / 1 s).

**Paper claims — data (§3):** custom multivariate time-series dataset built from TennisTV YouTube videos of the Vienna Open (indoor hard, singles points, each clip beginning at serve until a miss). Videos are converted to frames at ~60 fps. Each frame carries player bounding boxes, 17 2D keypoints per player, and ball X,Y. The paper states the dataset "is available for research" but reports **no** counts (no number of videos, frames, serves, players, or train/test split) — see limits below.

**Paper claims — method (§4.1–§4.4, Figures 3–5):**

- §4.1 Player detection: Faster R-CNN, `person` class, per-frame box proposals via RPN + CNN classifier/regressor (Figure 3).
- §4.2 Ball detection: TrackNet heatmap model at 640×360, frame-or-sequence input (Figure 3). Serves cited as ~120 mph men / ~105 mph women (via `tennicreative` website) to motivate a high-performance tiny-object tracker.
- §4.3 Pose: per-player crop resized to 224×224, fed to ViTPose (ViT backbone) yielding seventeen 2D keypoints (X,Y each: elbows, wrists, knees, etc.) (Figure 3).
- §4.4 Predictor: encoder–decoder Transformer. Encoder input is a sequence of feature sets `S_i` of **74 values** per time step (player centroids + joint positions + ball positions); decoder predicts `S'_i` of **2 values** (future centroid X,Y). Training uses teacher forcing, Time2Vector time encoding (Eq. 1, concatenated to both encoder and decoder inputs), an added LSTM to smooth X/Y outputs (Figure 5), a causal decoder mask, 2 encoder layers (self-attention + feed-forward + norm), a 3-sublayer decoder (extra encoder-attention), MSE loss with Adam (β1=0.9, β2=0.98, ε=1e−9), dropout + weight decay, 30 epochs. Figure 4's worked example: 30-frame encoder context predicting 6 frames ahead; decoder input mixes recent encoder-covered frames with following frames.

**Paper claims — evaluation (§5, Table 1, Eq. 2, Figures 1/6):** metric is Mean Euclidean Distance Error (MEDE) in pixels between predicted and ground-truth centroids, averaged over T future steps (Eq. 2). Four model families × three training lengths × seven prediction horizons are reported for "the first player" (Table 1):

- Family 1: trajectory only (worst overall).
- Family 2: trajectory + all body joints (best at short horizons 50/100/150 ms; bold in Table 1; degrades on long horizons).
- Family 3: Family 2 features + occlusion/causality mask (best at 200/250 ms; paper attributes this to forced causal prediction).
- Family 4: trajectory + joints of both players + ball position + mask (best at 500 ms and 1 s: **48 px** and **67 px** respectively under the 500 ms-training row).

The paper's operational reading (§5): error ≤ ~48 px at 30 frames (500 ms) keeps the player inside the 224×224 follow box (Figure 1). Figure 6 plots Family 4 (500 ms training) X/Y predictions vs. ground truth at 250 ms / 500 ms / 1 s.

**Paper claims — author interpretation (§§5.1, 6):** joint information is "essential" (Family 2 > Family 1 at short range); masking helps long-range robustness; ball position captures player–ball coupling and helps longest horizons (Family 4 best at 500 ms/1 s). Kalman/SORT-style trackers and centroid-only extrapolation are discussed in Related Work (§2) as adequate only for short-term prediction and explicitly rejected for 0.5–1 s forecasting. Conclusion (§6) frames results as "promising" for camera auto-tracking; no coaching, scoring, or biomechanical claims are made.

## 2. Data / method / model / evaluation limits (paper claims unless noted)

- **No dataset scale or split.** §3 gives source (TennisTV Vienna Open), frame rate (~60 fps), and content (boxes + 17 keypoints + ball) but no N videos/frames/players/serves, no resolution, no annotation protocol for joints, no train/validation/test definition, and no held-out-tournament test. *Project inference:* Table 1 cannot be read as a generalization result; it is a single-dataset error table for one predicted player.
- **Heavy manual/semi-manual cleanup.** §3 reports three failure modes and fixes: (a) non-player persons (spectators/referee/ball kids) — median-filter background estimation failed under motion, Harris-corner court-line detection + per-frame Euclidean distance to nearest court point was used instead; (b) Faster R-CNN missed athletic poses (Figure 2; COCO pre-training lacks sports images) — a GUI script triaged bad boxes into a separate file for manual re-labeling in AWS SageMaker; (c) ball out-of-frame for 100+ frames on lobs — polynomial regression from 10 points before/after the gap synthesized replacements. *Project inference:* the "high-quality dataset" label is a paper claim resting on manual repair; the pipeline as described is not fully automatic.
- **Pixel-space, broadcast-camera metric.** MEDE (Eq. 2) is raw pixels with no normalization by player scale, torso length, image resolution, or court geometry, and no confidence intervals. A 48 px error means different things on different zooms. Broadcast cameras pan/tilt/zoom, so image-plane centroid motion confounds player locomotion with camera motion — never addressed. *Project inference:* MEDE numbers must not be compared to our source-time (seconds) phase errors.
- **Table 1 ambiguities.** The third training-length row in every family is printed "**1 ms**" (almost certainly a "**1 s**" typo, given the column header "1 s" and the 500 ms / 750 ms siblings); the "1 s" predicting-horizon column header vs. row labels is likewise easy to misread. No standard deviations, significance tests, or per-horizon sample counts are given. Bold marks only Family 2 short-horizon cells; Family 3/4 "best" claims for longer horizons are textual (§5.1), not bolded.
- **No ablations for key components.** Time2Vector (Eq. 1), the LSTM smoother (Figure 5, qualitative X/Y curves only), teacher forcing, dropout/weight-decay values, and the "mask" (occlusion vs. causal — see below) have no numeric ablations. The 74-feature composition is stated but not itemized per contributor.
- **"Mask" is under-specified.** §5 says Family 3 adds "a mask to account for occlusions," while §5.1 describes masking as "preventing information leakage … forced to learn … based on information available at each time step" (i.e., causal masking, also mentioned in §4.4 as "decoder mask"). Whether one or two distinct masks are used, and how occlusion flags are computed from Faster R-CNN/ViTPose/TrackNet outputs, is never defined. *Project inference:* do not cite this as an occlusion-handling specification; at most it corroborates that explicit missingness handling helps.
- **No baselines despite discussing them.** §2 reviews Kalman filters, SORT/DeepSORT/FairMOT/ByteTrack/BoT-SORT, and prior trajectory-forecasting work, but §5 compares only the four Transformer families against each other — no Kalman, linear-extrapolation, or centroid-only-heuristic baseline numbers.
- **Single-player, singles-only, serve-agnostic.** Only "the first player" is predicted/evaluated; doubles, the second player as target, and any serve-vs-rally breakdown are absent. Clips merely *start* at serves; no serve-phase labels, serve-type labels, or contact events exist. Ball–racket contact, racket pose, and swing classification are cited as related work (§2), not attempted here.
- **Interpolation as ground truth.** Ball gaps of 100+ frames are filled by polynomial regression and then used as both input features and (implicitly) training context — a form of synthetic supervision whose error is never quantified.

## 3. Findings relevant to pose features (angles / speeds) and phase / event detection

### 3.1 Pose features: joints help, but no angles or speeds are studied

- **Paper claims:** the *only* pose representation is raw 2D keypoint X,Y (17 per player via ViTPose on 224×224 crops, §4.3) plus centroid X,Y and ball X,Y (§4.4). No joint angles (elbow/knee/hip/coil), no angular or linear velocities, no accelerations, no arc length, no smoothing windows in seconds, and no visibility/quality channels are defined. Derivatives exist only implicitly inside the Transformer's sequence context. Feature importance over joints is never reported, so "how crucial are the various body parts" (§1's motivating question) is **not** answered joint-by-joint — only the coarse Family 1-vs-2 gap addresses it.
- **Paper claims (Table 1):** adding all joints to trajectory history cuts short-horizon error roughly in half (e.g., 500 ms-training row: 79→36 px @50 ms; 95→50 @100 ms; 110→47 @150 ms), but joints alone do not hold up at long horizons (Family 2 @1 s: 109/119/147 px across training rows — worse than or comparable to Family 1's 93/142/124).
- **Project inference:** this corroborates our standing design choice (M4.2/remediation §4) that *change-aware, multi-cue* descriptors beat single-cue static ones — but it provides zero validation for our specific angle definitions (elbow/knee flexion, shoulder/hip tilt, shoulder–hip separation), our second-based Savitzky–Golay windows, or our velocity/acceleration formulation. The paper's lesson is directional (pose context > centroid alone at short range; pose alone insufficient at long range) and must not be cited as support for any M4 threshold, weight, or window length.

### 3.2 Speeds: only implicit sequence dynamics; explicit smoothing is qualitative

- **Paper claims:** ball speed motivates TrackNet (§4.2) but is never estimated from pose. Player "speed" is never computed. The LSTM addition is justified verbally (retain long-term dependencies, reduce noise, capture trend) with Figure 5 X/Y smoothness curves and no error delta. Time2Vector (Eq. 1, Fourier time embedding per Kazemi et al. [59]) is the only explicit time handling, concatenated to encoder and decoder inputs.
- **Project inference:** the closest M4 analogue is our timestamp-aware derivatives + physical-delta smoothing (M4.2) and our `start`/`finish` velocity-run rules plus `acceleration`/`deceleration` 50%-arc-length midpoints (remediation §3). The paper is circumstantial corroboration for *preferring change-based descriptors over static extrema*, which the remediation plan already adopts. It offers no usable speed estimator, no window constant, and no evidence about 30 Hz vs. 120 Hz cadence (its data are ~60 fps broadcast frames).

### 3.3 Phase / event detection: none — this is trajectory forecasting, not phase timing

- **Paper claims:** there are no phases, stages, events, intervals, keyframes, ordering constraints, or timing-error metrics anywhere in the paper. No `start`/`release`/`loading`/`cocking`/`acceleration`/`contact`/`deceleration`/`finish` equivalent; no toss, no loading peak, no contact anchor (audio or visual); no DP/HMM/decoder over stages. Output is a dense future centroid track, not a sparse event set. Eval horizons (50 ms–1 s, Table 1) are *prediction lead times for camera steering*, not stage durations or onset errors.
- **Project inference:** this paper contributes nothing directly to eight-stage Kovacs sequencing, contact anchoring, or phase-evaluation methodology. Its one transferable timing observation is operational: the authors argue cameras need ≥0.5 s (ideally 1 s) of look-ahead given ~10 requests/s actuation limits (§1) — a camera-control latency point, not a phase-timing result. Do not cite Table 1 as a phase-accuracy reference.

### 3.4 Contact, release, ball–racket claims: present only as inputs, never as predictions

- **Paper claims:** ball X,Y (TrackNet, §4.2, with polynomial infill, §3) is an *input* that improves long-horizon centroid prediction (Family 4 @500 ms: 48 px vs. Family 3's 83 and Family 2's 91 on the 500 ms-training row; @1 s: 67 vs. 86/109). Ball–racket impact is never detected, timestamped, or evaluated. Toss release never appears. Racket position/orientation never appears (racket work is §2 related work only).
- **Project inference:** the Family 4 gain is the paper's strongest argument that *contextual coupling signals* (here, ball position) extend effective horizon — but under our contract that channel is forbidden in M4 (remediation §8 defers all ball/racket work to M5-or-later) and our `contact` stays an audio-anchored uncertain interval while `release` stays an explicit body proxy (M4.1, remediation §3). This paper must not be cited as evidence for any visual-contact or ball-derived release rule.

## 4. What M4 can adopt or evaluate (no new models / dependencies / ball–racket claims)

All items below fit the M4.1–M4.6 + remediation allowed files (feature/evidence/solver diagnostics and report wording) and require no new packages, no learned weights, and no ball/racket observation. None changes M3 outputs.

1. **Keep multi-cue composites; treat Family 1-vs-2 as weak corroboration.** *Paper claims:* trajectory-only (Family 1) is worst at every horizon/length in Table 1; adding joints (Family 2) halves short-horizon error. *Project inference:* when the phase-debug artifact (remediation §5) is built, check that no body stage is decided by a single global extremum; require ≥2 visibility-qualified cues per stage per M4.3. Do not import any numeric weight from this paper (none exists).
2. **Retain explicit missingness/occlusion channels (M4.2 masks).** *Paper claims:* the masked Family 3 beats unmasked Family 2 at 200/250 ms and at most longer horizons (e.g., 500 ms-training: 71→53 @200 ms, 84→64 @250 ms, 91→83 @500 ms), attributed to blocking future leakage and handling occlusions. *Project inference:* this supports our existing rule — short visibility-qualified interpolation only, long gaps forbidden, interpolated points never sole support for a high-confidence keyframe, observation/quality/interpolation-span channels retained. The paper's mask definition is too vague to copy; keep our explicit binary observation mask semantics.
3. **Prefer physical-time handling over frame-count handling.** *Paper claims:* Time2Vector time embeddings (Eq. 1) are concatenated to encoder *and* decoder inputs; smoothing/derivatives are discussed in physical terms (ms horizons, 60 fps source). *Project inference:* consistent with remediation §4 (timestamp deltas, second-based windows, exact presentation timestamps, never `frame / assumed_fps`). No constant to import; a process alignment only.
4. **Keep change-based transitional descriptors.** *Paper claims:* static/joint context wins short-range but decays; ball-coupled sequence context wins long-range (Family 4). *Project inference:* circumstantial support for our `acceleration`/`deceleration` wrist-arc midpoints and `start`/`finish` velocity-run rules over single-frame extrema. Verify via the debug artifact that endpoint anchors exist before deriving midpoints (remediation §6 already requires this).
5. **Adopt strict error-vs.-tolerance reporting in private diagnostics.** *Paper claims:* §5 judges 48 px error against the concrete 224×224 follow-box tolerance and plots X/Y vs. ground truth per horizon (Figure 6). *Project inference:* mirror the habit in debug notes — e.g., plot selected vs. manual keyframe per stage with contact-relative offsets and observation support (remediation §5 already requires this), optionally with a trivial contact-centered naive baseline to avoid fooling ourselves on easy stages. Do not change the committed gate (availability, accepted-keyframe rate, signed/absolute error, P90, order violations, calibration).
6. **Treat manual-QC rates as justification for visibility gating, not as a labeling recipe.** *Paper claims:* Faster R-CNN sport-pose misses (Figure 2) required GUI triage + SageMaker relabeling; ball gaps required polynomial infill. *Project inference:* do not synthesize observations across long gaps (our M4.2 already forbids it); keep `partial`/`unavailable` honest states with stable anomaly IDs rather than forcing complete sequences — the paper's infill is exactly the anti-pattern our solver's skip states (M4.4) are designed to avoid.

## 5. What cannot fit M4 (and why)

- **Encoder–decoder Transformer predictor (§4.4).** Learned sequence models are out of M4 scope (roadmap M4.1–M4.4 mandate deterministic evidence + DP solver; remediation §8 defers all training). A Transformer needs training infrastructure, held-out tuning, and a dataset we do not have — none of which the narrow heuristic/chronology repair may introduce.
- **ViTPose / Faster R-CNN / TrackNet pipeline (§§4.1–4.3).** M4 remediation pins the existing approved MediaPipe Heavy body model + raw-source audio (remediation §1). New detectors/pose backends need new dependencies and a Core ML path per design §6; the roadmap only permits dependency additions in the ticket that first uses them. Nothing in Table 1 justifies swapping pose backends for rear-view 120 fps iPhone footage (paper data are ~60 fps broadcast YouTube).
- **Ball-position input channel (Family 4, §§3/4.2).** Ball tracking is explicitly deferred (remediation §8). The paper's ball inputs are themselves partly polynomial-synthesized (§3), so they cannot ground a body-only M4 claim anyway.
- **Trajectory forecasting as an output.** Design §2 scopes M4 to optional phase anchors with uncertainty inside accepted attempts; predicted future centroids, follow-boxes, and camera steering (§1/§5) are not M4 outputs. Adding them would be scope creep plus an unevaluated generalization claim.
- **Pixel-error targets (Table 1 MEDE).** Image-pixel errors from broadcast footage do not transfer to source-second phase errors on 120 fps slow motion under a rear-view contract. Quoting 36–67 px as an M4 target would be a category error.
- **Polynomial infill of long gaps (§3).** Directly contradicts M4.2/remediation §4 (never bridge configured long gaps; uncertainty never narrower than supporting observation spacing). Forbidden as a repair technique.
- **Speed estimation, coaching, technique scoring.** The paper attempts none of these (its speed mentions are input-velocity motivation, §4.2); design §2 and M4.1 exclude them regardless.
- **Contact-as-visual-frame or release-as-ball-event.** The paper provides no such detector; M4.1 forbids claiming them from body pose, and remediation §3 keeps `contact` audio-anchored with uncertainty and `release`/`cocking` as labeled proxies.

## 6. What belongs in deferred M5 (and why)

Per `docs/m5-tcn-phase-detection.md` (proposal) and remediation §8:

- **Learned temporal dynamics over dense native features (M5 §3 TCN + DP decoder).** *Why M5:* the paper's core forward lesson is that raw sequence context + coupling signals beat hand-extrema at longer horizons (Families 2→4, Table 1). Our M4 solver is fixed-form DP over hand-built evidence; any learned scorer (TCN *or* Transformer), learned duration priors, or modality dropout belongs in the separately gated M5 experiment with its data/labeling/eval apparatus — not in the narrow M4 repair. Note the architecture mismatch: M5 proposes heatmap-event TCN outputs; this paper predicts dense centroid tracks, so it motivates *sequence modeling in general*, not the M5 target formulation.
- **Ball (and racket) channels.** *Why M5-or-later:* Family 4's long-horizon gain is the paper's best evidence that ball context helps; M5 §3 is the first place ball position/velocity/confidence channels appear. Still gated separately and never automatic ground truth.
- **Multi-view / multi-player / multi-rate generalization.** *Why M5:* paper data are multi-player broadcast singles with camera motion and ~60 fps; our M4 contract is one right-handed rear-view player at native 120 fps. Generalizing across views, handedness, and 30–240 fps is M5 scope (M5 §§1–2), requiring the labeling and session-disjoint evaluation M4 must not attempt.
- **Camera auto-tracking / production use (§1 motivation).** *Why out-of-scope:* downstream application context only; neither M4 nor M5 proposes camera control.
- **Anything needing PyTorch, new pose/detection weights, training data, or dataset construction of this kind.** *Why M5-or-later:* remediation §8 names PyTorch/TCN training as deferred; the paper's GUI+SageMaker relabeling and polynomial infill are cautionary examples for M5 label design (proposed labels for review, never automatic ground truth).

If M4 succeeds narrowly, its dense 120 fps caches and diagnostics become *proposed* labels for M5 review (remediation §8) — the same status the paper's semi-manual dataset should have held.

## 7. Prioritized recommendations

1. **No M4 threshold, weight, or window changes from this paper.** It has no angles, no velocities, no phase decomposition, and no transferable constants — our failure is eight-stage chronology on 120 fps rear-view slow motion, not centroid tracking on broadcast video. Changing evidence weights from Table 1 would be tuning to an incomparable task.
2. **Use the paper once, as a debug-artifact sanity check:** confirm `loading`/`cocking` candidates respond to multi-cue body composites (knee/elbow/wrist-pose context) rather than a single extremum, and that long-gap stages go `partial`/`unavailable` instead of being infilled. If they do not, the fault is in candidate generation or skip scoring (remediation §5) — no paper constants needed.
3. **Keep the arc-length-midpoint `acceleration`/`deceleration` and velocity-run `start`/`finish` definitions.** The paper's only durable signal (context/change beats static) is the closest available corroboration for change descriptors over static extrema.
4. **Do not adopt TrackNet ball inputs, ViTPose/Faster R-CNN backends, Transformer scoring, polynomial infill, or pixel-error targets in M4.** Each violates the narrow contract or the no-unsupported-claims rule; the paper itself withholds the ablations and split definitions that would justify them.
5. **File narrow M5 pointers:** sequence-model dynamics, ball channels, and cross-view/rate generalization go to M5; cite this paper's Family 2→4 progression and its manual-cleanup caveats as motivation, not as a specification.
